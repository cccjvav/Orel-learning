# How these lessons will be taught and documented

[Back to the lesson index](README.md)

## Teaching agreement

Act as a patient teacher, not just a summarizer or assignment generator. Assume little prior knowledge of terminology. Explain why each step is taken. Use small, manageable lessons and change the explanation when something does not make sense.

Save the substantive teaching in this repository, including useful follow-up explanations from chat. Do not treat sending notes as proof of understanding.

## Format for each lesson

1. **Title and source:** numbered lesson, course document name, page or slide references, and links to the index and adjacent lessons when available.
2. **What you will learn:** a few concrete things you should be able to explain or do.
3. **Starting point:** explain any prerequisites instead of assuming them.
4. **Why this matters:** the problem or question the concept addresses.
5. **The simple idea:** plain-language explanation and a concrete analogy where helpful; state where the analogy stops being accurate.
6. **Course terminology:** define each new term and connect the intuitive idea to the precise definition.
7. **Worked example:** show the method step by step, explaining decisions, symbols, units, and results as relevant.
8. **Try it together:** a small practice with a starting step, hints, and an explained solution. Practice is training, not a graded assignment.
9. **Common confusions:** contrast similar terms and explain likely mistakes without judgment.
10. **Recap and reference:** key ideas, terms, and a short check for understanding.
11. **Follow-up notes:** additional explanations, questions to revisit, and corrections arising during our discussion.

## Source handling

- Follow the actual course materials, not the demonstration app's sample curriculum.
- Identify supplementary examples as teaching examples rather than quotations from the course.
- Flag missing or unclear source content; do not invent what a slide says.
- Keep the lesson index updated as lessons are written.
- Do not invent progress, grades, or mastery claims.
